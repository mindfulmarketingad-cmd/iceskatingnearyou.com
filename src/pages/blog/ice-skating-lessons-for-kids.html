What age can kids start lessons

Snowplow Sam is four levels for children aged six and younger. Basic Skills is six levels for skaters over age 6. U.S. Figure Skating endorses the program with USA Hockey and US Speedskating, describes pathways for preschoolers, kids, adults, and skaters with disabilities, and says lessons run at over 1,000 programs. Learn to Skate USA membership is separate from a U.S. Figure Skating membership. A club is for later, when a skater wants official tests.

The youngest age a rink will take is local:

  • Burnsville, Minnesota, lists Skater Tots at 18 to 36 months, with a parent in the building and a 4-to-1 ratio. Snowplow Sam there is ages 3 to 5. Basic 1 starts at 6. The tot hour is play on the ice. Snowplow Sam is the four-level curriculum.
  • Rocky River, Ohio, lists Snowplow Sam for ages 4 to 6 and Basic Skills for ages 6 to 15.
  • Chicago's McCormick Tribune Ice Rink teaches any age in its free weekend lessons while that outdoor season is open.

A child who walks steadily and can follow a simple direction is ready to try. A child who will not can wait a season. The family guide has the longer readiness list.

Group classes and private lessons

The usual first enrollment is a group series: one level, other beginners, and the same weekly hour. Learn to Skate USA's parent handbook says skaters move up when they master a skill, that repeating a class is ordinary, and that a private coach can wait until one skill needs extra work. It tells parents to look past a championship resume when they hire for a beginner, and it mentions a booster lesson of about 15 minutes.

Burnsville prints its ratios: 10 students per instructor for Snowplow Sam, Basic 1, and hockey skating, and 6 per instructor for Basic 2 through 6. Ask any other rink for its cap, and where parents stand. The handbook says to watch from the bleachers, arrive about 20 minutes early, and have skates tied 15 minutes before class.

Private lessons on official pages come in two shapes. Mt. Shasta sells one 30-minute private lesson, and its group schedule was still to be determined on the 2026-27 fee page. Burnsville sells a 9-week private block for ages 3 and up, with 20 minutes of instruction and 40 minutes of practice. Semi-private there splits the fee when two or more skaters share a level. Amounts are in the table below. A coach off the city list may have no posted rate. Ask the skating director whether the coach is a steady role model, has a plan for the skill, and is easy to reach between lessons.

How Learn to Skate USA levels work

Membership is $20 from July 1 through June 30. The registration page includes the curriculum, a record book, the app, secondary sports accident insurance, professional instruction, and access to rinks and events. The parent page says some rinks fold that $20 into tuition and some have you pay Learn to Skate USA directly. Burnsville includes a year of membership in a 9-week series and leaves it out of Skater Tots.

Hockey learn-to-play programs

The Learn to Skate USA hockey track is four badges plus power skating: stance, stride, and knee bend, then speed and agility. The handbook says the next step is Intro to Hockey or Learn to Play, which adds stickhandling, shooting, and passing. Burnsville's Hockey Tracks class, ages 5 and up after Snowplow Sam 2, keeps sticks and pucks off the ice.

USA Hockey's Active Start paper (ages 0 to 6) says to start in learn-to-skate, preferably without a stick. Its first-year Learn to Play sketch is 50-minute sessions, once or twice a week, for 16 weeks, with 9 to 13 skaters, no goalies, and no formal competition. Coaches need Level 1 USA Hockey Coaching Education Program certification. The 6-and-under structure is for ages 4 to 6 after that base: stations of at most 6 to 8 children, about six minutes each, with the coach at eye level.

Rocky River Level 1, for 2026-27, is ages 4 to 6 who finished Snowplow Sam and can skate the width of the ice alone. The city lends equipment. Levels 2 and 3 require the player's own skates and stick, and the city says USA Hockey requires neck guards under 18. Fees are in the table below. Try Hockey for Free lists October 3, 2026 and February 27, 2027, for a first hour with skates, a helmet, and gloves (a bike helmet and winter gloves are allowed), promoted to ages 4 to 9. Come Play Youth Hockey collects those options. Chicago's free hockey-skills group is for skaters who already use hockey skates. Beginners use the beginner lesson that same morning.

What to wear and bring

  • Thin socks. The handbook says thick socks bunch and reduce support. How ice skates should fit covers the heel and the laces.
  • Gloves or mittens. Falling is the first skill. Burnsville requires gloves at every lesson.
  • A helmet for beginners. The handbook strongly recommends one, worn level with the strap buckled. Burnsville strongly recommends helmets for its youngest classes and Basic 1, and requires them for hockey. Rocky River requires them for every Snowplow Sam level. Chicago strongly encourages them for beginners.
  • Clothes that bend. The handbook suggests warm-up pants and a jacket, and says indoor rinks run around 50 degrees. Burnsville strongly recommends snow pants for the youngest classes because those skaters sit on the ice. Follow the rink if it names snow pants. What to wear ice skating covers layers.
  • Rental skates first. The handbook says to rent, buy later, and use hard guards off the ice. Burnsville includes rentals, strongly recommends figure skates for a first lesson, and does not recommend double-bladed skates. Rocky River allows hockey or figure skates in Basic Skills.

How to judge a program and a coach

Five questions sort a good class from a vague one. Which Learn to Skate USA level is this, and where are new skaters placed? Burnsville does that by phone. What is the ratio, next to Burnsville's 10-to-1 beginners or USA Hockey's 6-to-8 children per station? Which credential does the instructor hold? The handbook requires Learn to Skate USA training, testing, and a background screening. USA Hockey requires Level 1 coaching certification for the youngest hockey players. May a child repeat a level until the eight-out-of-ten standard, or does everyone move up on a calendar? And where do parents stand? The handbook says the bleachers. Chicago requires a registered lesson before a parent steps on the ice. A private coach should come through the skating director, with a plan for the skill and a way to reach them. If you can watch, look for moving children, a named skill, and more than one turn apiece.

How to find lessons near you

  1. Use the national finder. Learn to Skate USA's program finder takes a ZIP code and lists programs on this curriculum.
  2. Read the rink's own class page. Cities post age, fee, and helmet rules on recreation sites the finder does not replace.
  3. Start from a state hub. Browse by state. Wide directories include Minnesota, New York, Massachusetts, and California.
  4. Filter the directory. Rinks that mention lessons are tagged from their own listings. Slices with several matches include California, Texas, Maryland, and Illinois. A missing rink may still teach. Then try hockey rinks, figure skating rinks, or skate rentals. Search when you know the city.
  5. Pick ice that lasts the series. An indoor arena can finish a nine-week January class. A seasonal sheet may close with the weather, which is why Millennium Park's free lessons end January 31. Indoor rinks are the steadier place to finish Basic 1.

Ask the age cutoff, the level name, the weeks and minutes, whether rentals and the $20 membership are included, the helmet rule, and the missed-class policy. Register for the level the child can pass.
