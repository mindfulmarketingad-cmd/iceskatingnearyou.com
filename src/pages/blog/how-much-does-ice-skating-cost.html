A casual skate is usually two charges, not one. You pay to get on the ice, and you pay again if you need rental skates. At a neighborhood rink those two lines can land in the same range as a movie ticket. At a famous holiday rink, the same afternoon can cost several times that, or the admission can be free and the rental can be the whole bill.

This guide uses prices published by the rinks and park departments themselves. Every dollar figure below was copied from the linked page on November 10, 2026. It is not a national average. Rinks that do not post a rate are left out, and a posted "from" price is the starting price, not the price of every session. Rates, taxes, and session times change. Check the rink before you go.

What you are actually paying for

Most public sessions sell a block of ice time. You skate until that session ends, then the rink clears the ice. A few seasonal rinks let you stay as long as you like during public hours. The receipt still tends to split the same way.

  • Admission is the right to skate that session. Many rinks charge less for children and seniors than for adults. Some also charge a spectator who walks into the rink area, and some let spectators in free.
  • Skate rental is usually separate. If the price says "with skates" or "includes skates," the rental is already in the number. If it does not, add it.
  • Skate aids, lockers, socks, and sharpening are extras. A first lesson for a small child can add a skate aid. A busy holiday rink can add a bag check.
  • Lessons and passes are different products. A learn-to-skate class is a set of weeks. A season pass or punch card prepays admission, and only some of them include rentals.

Before you compare two rinks, write down admission, rental, and how long you get on the ice. A $5 session that runs about two hours is a different purchase from a $22 ticket for 60 minutes.

How much ice skate rental costs

Rental is the fee people forget, and it is often as large as admission. At the community rinks above it is $3 or $6, or it is folded into a bundle. At the landmark outdoor rinks later in this guide it is $12, or a wide range that starts higher than that.

A few patterns show up on the pages that post a rental price:

  • Own skates usually skip the fee. Baltimore, Niles, Columbus, Mt. Shasta, Wollman, Rockefeller Center, and Bryant Park all let you bring skates. Millennium Park's reservation page is the exception to "free if you bring them": own-skates entry is free Monday through Thursday, and $5 on Fridays, weekends, and listed holidays, except the 11 a.m. session, which stays free every day.
  • The cheap rental is the basic pair for that session. It is not a promise about size range. Millennium Park lists youth size 8 through adult 14. Bryant Park lists kids 6T-7T, big kids 1-4, and adults 5-14. Wollman lists a toddler 8 through a men's 14. If you are outside that range, call before you go or bring your own.
  • Skate aids are extra, and sometimes free at quiet times. Baltimore lists them at $5. Millennium Park lists a skate-aide for skaters under 42 inches at $11, on site only. Wollman lists a skate aid at $25 for children 12 and younger who are under 4 feet. Bryant Park lists a skate aid at $26, including a $1 administrative fee, and says it is free before 10:30 a.m. when available.
  • Sharpening is a separate small bill if you own skates. Posted prices include $10 at Millennium Park, $11 at Longmont, $15 at Mt. Shasta for drop-off, $20 at Wollman, and $20.80 for a 24-hour turnaround at Bryant Park, or $41.60 while you wait. Those Bryant Park figures include the administrative fee the park lists. See how often to sharpen ice skates before you pay for a sharpen you do not need yet.

Rentals are a starting point, not a fitted boot. If the heel slips or the ankle folds, ask for another size. How ice skates should fit walks through that check. Rinks that mention rentals in their own listings are collected on rinks with skate rentals. A rink missing from that list may still rent skates. The tag is applied only when the listing says so.

Indoor rinks, outdoor rinks, and holiday pop-ups

The ice is the same idea. The bill is not.

Indoor public sessions

Indoor community rinks are the predictable, lower-priced option in this set. You are paying for a maintained sheet and a scheduled session, often year round or from early fall through late spring. Baltimore's $5 admission and $3 rental, Niles at $7 plus $6, and Mt. Shasta at $12 plus $3 are that kind of visit. The tradeoff is the clock. Public skate is fitted around hockey and lessons, so the cheap session may be a weekday midday block. Weekend afternoons fill first.

Outdoor park rinks

Outdoor park ice is seasonal and weather dependent. Longmont's drop-in, which includes skates, sits in the same price band as the indoor rinks: $12 for a resident adult and $14 for a nonresident adult in 2026-27. The city says reservations are not used for open skate. You still need a cold enough stretch for the ice to stay open. The outdoor ice skating guide explains how this site decides which listings count as outdoor, and it tells you to confirm hours and prices with the rink.

Wollman Rink in Central Park is also outdoor and seasonal, and it prices like a destination. Adult admission (ages 13-64), for skating and non-skating guests, is $16 off-peak, $28 peak, and $40 prime. The rental stays $12 on every day type, and the rink says those prices include tax. An adult who rents skates is therefore $28 off-peak and $52 on a prime day, using the rink's own addition. Children 3 and under are free, and children 4-12 are $12 on every day type. Each of those child tickets includes one free non-skating chaperone age 21 or older.

Holiday pop-up and plaza rinks

Holiday rinks sell the place as much as the ice: a short timed session, a reservation, and a famous backdrop. That is why "how much is ice skating" spikes when Rockefeller Center and Bryant Park are in the news. The two Midtown rinks do it in opposite ways. Rockefeller Center sells a timed ticket that starts higher than a community admission. Bryant Park lists admission at $0 and sells the rental, which can cost more than a community rink's whole visit.

Pop-up ice is also the first to change hours for tree lightings, holidays, and warm weather. Treat a holiday listing as a plan, then open the rink's calendar the day you go. Christmas ice skating near New York and the seasonal rink listings are the place to start a search. Outdoor rinks are the broader set.

What famous rinks charge

These are the rinks people price-check first. The figures are starting prices or posted ranges. A sold-out Saturday in December will not match the cheapest line on the page.

The Rink at Rockefeller Center

The Rink's own ticket page, for the 2026-2027 season, lists General Skate from $22 and VIP Skate from $131. Both are 60-minute sessions. The page says the rink is open daily from 9 a.m. to midnight, with hours that vary, and that every guest needs a ticket, including someone who is not skating. You can bring skates or rent them. The ticket page does not print a standalone rental price.

Rockefeller Center's own guide, updated September 2026, fills in more of the menu. General admission starts at $22 for adults and children and $12 for ages five and under. Skate rental is $12. VIP Skate on that guide starts at $112 and includes the rental, priority ice access, warm drinks and snacks, and the heated lounge. Same-day Skate Now tickets, sold only at the box office when they are available, cost $25 to $40 more than the matching General Skate ticket.

Use both pages together, and trust the ticket calendar for the session you want. The live ticket page's VIP starting price ($131) is higher than the September guide's VIP starting price ($112). A "from" price is the floor. Weekends and the weeks around Christmas are the busy, costlier end, which is also what the rink tells people to book ahead for.

The same guide dates the season from October 10, 2026 through March 27, 2027, with the rebuilt Skate House opening November 14, and with the rink closed December 1 and 2 for the tree lighting. Holiday Chalets, a private rinkside reservation for up to six guests rather than a normal public ticket, run November 6 through January 2. The guide says private chalet bookings start at $70, for 50 minutes, with a $25 per person food and beverage minimum. That is a different product from General Skate.

If the $22 starting ticket plus a $12 rental is more than you want for an hour, skate somewhere else in the city and visit the tree on foot. Ice rinks near New York lists sheets within 30 miles of downtown, including ordinary public sessions.

Bryant Park's free admission, paid rental

Bank of America Winter Village at Bryant Park is the other search that jumps every November. The park's rink page says admission is free, and that the rink is open daily from October 28, 2026 through February 28, 2027, weather permitting. It is a 17,000 square foot sheet. If you bring your own skates, the park says you can skate for free. You still need a reserved entry time. Own-skates reservations are released daily for dates one week out, and the park asks for one session per person per day.

The rental is where the visit starts costing money. The park lists skate rentals at $18.72 to $64.48, including a $0.72 to $2.48 administrative fee, and it says the prices do not include tax. It also says off-peak times cost less. On select dates, an Express Lane ticket with rental skates is $87.52, including a $3.37 administrative fee, and it lengthens the skate to 75 minutes. Express Lane with your own skates is $18.42 to $36.86. Premium Skate, with a concierge, a snack and drink, the shared Polar Lounge, rentals, bag check, and longer ice time, is $156 to $182.

Almost every standard session is 50 minutes of ice, and your ice time starts 20 minutes after the ticket time so you can lace up. Helmet rental is free. Shoe check is free. A bag check is $6.24 per item, or $18.72 for an oversized item such as a stroller, again including the small administrative fee the park prints. A Bank of America credit or debit card takes 10 percent off eligible online skate rentals between October 28, 2026 and February 28, 2027, up to $500 in rentals, if you enter the card on the park's checkout page.

So "free skating at Bryant Park" is true for admission, and it is a $0 visit only when you bring skates and get a reservation. A rental on a busy holiday slot can cost more than admission plus rental at Wollman on an off-peak day. Go earlier in the day, or on a weekday outside the November 19 to January 3 midnight hours, if you want the low end of that rental range.

What lessons cost

A public session is practice. A lesson is instruction, usually sold by the week or in a multi-week set, and the better pages say whether skates and a bit of practice ice are included. Rinks that list lessons are a starting map. The price is always on the rink's own registration page.

  • Baltimore group classes. Mt. Pleasant lists a 6-week learn-to-skate session at $100 and 5-week sessions at $84. Each price covers a 30-minute lesson, 30 minutes of practice, three passes to a public session, and rental skates. Spread evenly, the $84 session is $16.80 a week.
  • Mt. Shasta private lessons. The district lists a private lesson at $30 for 30 minutes, including skate rental and admission to the public session. The group-lesson schedule was still marked "to be determined" on the 2026-27 fee page.
  • Millennium Park group lessons. Free, as described above, on weekend mornings while the outdoor season is open, with free rental, and with a 20-person cap per level.
  • Freestyle ice is not a beginner class. Baltimore charges $13 for a freestyle session and says it is for skaters at Free Skate 1 or above who bring their own skates. Beginners belong in public skate or a learn-to-skate class, which is also the point of the public session guide.

Private coaches who are not the rink's class often set their own rates, and many rinks do not publish them. If a page does not list a coaching fee, ask the rink. Do not assume the $30 Mt. Shasta lesson is what a coach charges anywhere else. First-timers who want to practice between classes can use how to ice skate for beginners.

Season passes and punch cards

A pass pays off only after you beat the single-visit price. Divide the pass by the cost of the visits you would actually make, and include rental if the pass does not.

  • Siskiyou Ice Rink, Mt. Shasta. The 2026-27 adult season pass is $175. Youth and senior passes are $145. A family pass is $310, plus $35 for each additional youth, for up to four people at one address and no more than two adults. The district says the pass includes skate rentals, admission to all public skate sessions, and group lessons. An adult who would otherwise pay $12 plus $3 rental is spending $15 a visit. Twelve of those visits would be $180, which is more than the $175 pass, and the pass also includes the group lessons. A family that skates together a dozen times is the case the family pass is built for. Someone who will go twice should buy single sessions.
  • IceLand, Niles. A public-skate season pass is $150 for a resident and $180 for a nonresident, valid December 2026 through November 2027, and the district says fees are not prorated. A resident family of four is $300, and a nonresident family of four is $350, with each additional person at $50 or $63. The public-skate fee on the schedule is $7, and rental is listed separately at $6. The pass page describes it as a pass for public skate sessions and does not say rental is included, so ask before you assume the $6 is covered. On admission alone, 21 visits at $7 would be $147, still under the $150 pass, and 22 visits would be $154. If you will skate less often than that, single tickets cost less. Buy it in December if you can, because a pass bought in March still costs full price and expires in November.
  • Baltimore frequent-skater card. $50 for 12 public admissions, reloadable, with no expiration. It does not include rental skates. Twelve admissions at the $5 walk-up price would be $60, so the card saves $10 on admission across those 12 visits. It is a small discount for a regular, not a way to cut the bill in half. Rental is still $3 each time.
  • Longmont punch passes. For 10 or more skaters, a 10-19 visit punch pass is $9 per visit for residents and $10.25 for nonresidents, including rental. A 20-30 visit pass is $8.75 or $10. Separate skate-rental punch passes, for people who already have admission covered, are $3.50 a visit. The resident drop-in price with skates is $12 for an adult, so the 10-visit punch is the discount for groups and repeat skaters, not a second admission fee on top.

Read the exclusions. Longmont says recreation passes are not accepted for drop-in hockey. Niles does not prorate. Baltimore's freestyle card is a different product from the public-skate card, at $150 for 15 freestyle sessions.

Ways to spend less

The cheapest skate in this set is a free-admission rink, your own skates, and an off-peak reservation. The next cheapest is a city rink on a weekday.

  • Bring your own skates. That removes $3 to $6 at the community rinks in the table, $12 at Wollman and in the Rockefeller Center guide, and the entire Bryant Park rental range. You still pay admission where admission exists. You also take on sharpening a few times a season, at the $10 to $20 prices above. What to wear ice skating covers the thin socks that make your own boots, or a rental, feel right.
  • Use a rink that posts free admission. Bryant Park's admission is free for 2026-27 if you can reserve, including with your own skates. Millennium Park's admission is free, with the own-skates rules above. Columbus lets spectators in free, which matters if one adult is only there to watch. Wollman's child 3 and under is free and brings one adult chaperone in free.
  • Go off-peak. Wollman's adult price is $16, $28, or $40 for the same rink and the same $12 rental. Bryant Park says off-peak rentals are the low end of $18.72 to $64.48. Millennium Park's weekday rental is $18 against $22 on weekends and holidays. Rockefeller Center's own guide points locals toward mornings, and toward the last two hours before closing, because those sessions are calmer. Calmer often means the cheaper ticket is still available.
  • Ask about a local discount you already qualify for. Wollman's WRAP rate is $16 with skates and cocoa for a physical IDNYC or library card, walk-up only. Columbus lists military and first responders at $10 including rental on its 2026-27 table. Mt. Shasta lists active military admission at $9, with the $3 rental still separate. Bryant Park's Bank of America card discount is 10 percent off eligible rentals. These are specific programs, not a general "everyone gets a discount."
  • Share a locker and skip the add-ons. Wollman lockers are $8. Bryant Park's bag check starts at $6.24, and shoe check there is already free. Travel light and you can skip both.
  • Take the free lesson if you are in Chicago that weekend. Millennium Park's Saturday and Sunday 9 a.m. class includes the skate rental. Arrive by 8:45 a.m. and register the Monday before. Spots are limited to 20 per level.

Going with children adds the skate aid and, at some rinks, a rule that a grown-up has to be on the ice too. Ice skating with kids covers helmets, first visits, and how long a session is worth it. A short, cheap weekday session is usually a better first visit than a prime-time holiday ticket.

A simple way to budget the visit

Add three lines, then stop. Admission, rental if you need it, and one extra you know you will use, such as a skate aid or a locker. Ignore food until you are there. Holiday rinks sell a lot of cocoa, and that money is optional.

Check the rink before you go

Every figure in this guide can move. Holiday calendars add prime days. A warm week closes outdoor ice. A rink can leave last year's paragraph on the page under this year's table, the way Columbus still does. When you open a rink site, look for the season year, whether tax is included, and whether the price is admission only.

Then look up the sheet you can actually reach. Browse rinks by state, search the directory, or start from New York and Chicago if you are pricing those trips. The directory lists locations, hours, and phone numbers. It does not set the admission. The rink does.
